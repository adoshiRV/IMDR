# Korea — Six-Question Macro Note (June 2026)

Date: 2026-06-05
Author: ad@rvcapital

Grounded in IMDR `econ.fact_indicator` (172 KR series, BoK + KOSTAT + REB
+ FRED back to 2015) and the 48 Korea sell-side reports indexed in

## Q1. Saving & investment cycle since 2015 — where is 2026 vs prior episodes

**S − I identity says it loudly: CA surplus at multi-year high, but for
the wrong reason — I is collapsing, not S rising.**

| Year | CA (USD bn) | Goods bal | Facilities Inv YoY | Construction Inv YoY | Gov. gross saving (KRW trn) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | 105 | 120 | +4.9% | +7.3% | 109 |
| 2017 | 75 | 114 | **+15.8%** (chip cycle) | +7.9% | 143 |
| 2019 | 60 | 80 | −6.1% | −1.6% | 133 |
| 2021 | 84 | 80 | +10.3% | −0.3% | n/a |
| 2022 | **23** (oil-shock low) | 18 | −0.2% | −3.6% | 118 |
| 2024 | 100 | 111 | +1.8% | −3.1% | 90 |
| 2025 | **123** (record) | **138** | +2.1% | **−9.8%** | n/a |
| 2026 YTD (Q1) | 74 in one quarter | 74 in one quarter | **+3.6% YoY, +4.8% QoQ** | −1.4% YoY, +2.8% QoQ | — |

Pattern: 2026 looks like **2017 redux on the cap-ex / chip side, but
2022-23-style on construction**. Government gross saving has actually
deteriorated from 154trn (2018 peak) to 90trn (2024) — fiscal saving is
shrinking just as private-sector S/I gap is widening. The "China hoarding
2015–18" Korea analogue this cycle is the chip super-cycle without the
construction tag-along.

**2026 vs past episodes**:
- The 2025 CA of $123 bn already exceeds every prior peak (2015 was 105).
  2026 Q1 alone at $74 bn annualises to $200+ bn — would be the largest
  in the series.
- BUT — the *quality* is poor: this is depressed-investment surplus, not
  productivity-driven. Construction investment cumulative −15% over
  2022–25 (worse than 2018–19 cycle which only managed −5%).
- Facilities investment +4.8% QoQ in 2026Q1 is the cleanest signal yet
  that the *capex* side of I is starting to turn (Goldman/Nomura/BNP all
  flagged chip-led upturn in late-May–June reports). If construction
  stops bleeding in H2, Korea moves from depressed-I CA surplus to
  balanced-cycle CA surplus — equivalent to 2017.

## Q2. FX → inflation: domestic vs imported, services vs tradables

**Korea's empirical FX pass-through (using import-price indices, KRW vs
USD basis, 2018–26 monthly data):**

The two BoK import-price series let us mechanically decompose:
- *Import price USD basis* = world price move
- *Import price KRW basis* = world price + FX
- *Difference* = pure FX pass-through to imported-goods prices

| Episode | KRW move | USD imp-price move | KRW imp-price move | Implied FX pass-through to imp-prices |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 reflation (Jan21→Dec21) | −7.7% | +15.9% | +25.0% | ~9 pp (~115% pass-through to imp prices, near 1:1) |
| 2022 oil shock (Jan22→Oct22) | −20% | +10% | +18% | ~8 pp |
| 2024–25 Yoon shock (Sep24→Mar25) | −9.6% | −2.8% | +5.6% | ~8 pp |
| 2026 March spike (Feb26→Mar26) | −3.0% MoM | +15.0% MoM | +18.1% MoM | ~3 pp |

**Pass-through from KRW to import prices is close to 1:1** — that's the
mechanical link. Empirically Korean economists work with **CPI
pass-through coefficient ~0.07** (i.e. a 10% KRW depreciation adds 0.7pp
to CPI over 12 months), which lines up with what we see: KRW depreciated
~12% Sep 2024 → Mar 2026, headline CPI moved from 1.6% to 3.1% — but the
bulk of that 1.5pp move is energy/imports, not core.

**Services vs tradables split (KOSTAT core 2024.04 → 2026.05):**
- Core (ex-food + energy) has been **stuck at 2.0–2.5% for 18 months** —
  almost no FX feed-through. Services CPI is sticky around 2-2.5%.
- The May 2026 spike from 2.6% → 3.1% headline came almost entirely from
  energy/food-volatile, not core (core only moved 2.2 → 2.5).
- Conclusion: in Korea, FX pass-through is heavily concentrated in
  **goods/tradables**, with virtually no second-round effects into
  services until the wage-bargaining channel kicks in (we don't see that
  in 2025-26 data).
- BoK's own internal models (per BNP and Nomura's BoK-preview notes
  28-May) flag inflation expectations stability at ~2.0% as the reason
  they're not panicking despite the FX hit.

**Domestic vs imported share of inflation**:
- The PPI-services vs PPI-mfg split is the cleanest read. Services PPI
  has trended ~2-3% YoY in 2024-26; manufacturing PPI moved from 122
  (Mar 2025) → 132 (Mar 2026) → 138 (Apr 2026), or +13% in 12 months.
  **Tradables/imported is the dominant inflation force currently;
  domestic services is dormant**. This is the inverse of the US
  2022-24 sticky-services problem.

## Q3. Oil → CPI/GDP lag

**Import-price USD index proxies world-commodity input** (oil/gas
dominate Korea's imports — ~25% of import basket). Lining it up against
headline CPI:

| Oil/imp-price USD shock | Peak imp-price USD month | Headline CPI peak | Lag |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2018 oil run | Oct 2018 (120.1) | Oct/Nov 2018 (2.0%) | ~1 month coincident at headline |
| 2021–22 oil shock | Oct 2021–Jun 2022 | **Jul 2022 (6.3%)** | **3-6 months** from shock peak |
| 2023 base effects | Roll-off | Jun 2023 trough (2.7%) | Symmetric on the way down |
| 2026 spike | Mar 2026 (136.6) | May 2026 (3.1%, still rising) | 2 months and counting |

**Pattern**: Korean CPI pass-through from oil ≈ **2-3 months at the
wholesale (PPI) stage, 3-6 months at the headline-CPI level**, with
services largely insulated. The 2026 shock will likely peak through CPI
in Jul–Aug 2026 (3.3-3.8% headline) per Barclays' Jun-02 note ("first
hike in July") and Nomura's Jun-02 above-3% read.

**GDP effect** is harder to pin from this data alone, but the 2022 oil
shock provides the cleanest test:
- Oil shock peaks mid-22, GDP-imports YoY peaks Q3 2022, Korea Real GDP
  YoY falls from +3.2% (2022Q2) to +1.4% (2023Q1).
- The trade-balance hit was the proximate transmission — goods balance
  went $18.3 bn surplus in 2022 vs $80 bn in 2021. That's a ~3-4% of GDP
  terms-of-trade hit absorbed within 3 quarters.
- Income terms-of-trade (BOK.TOT.INCOME.LEVEL.KR) is the right indicator
  to monitor; it's the income loss from ToT moves.

**Headline elasticity**: ~10% oil-induced rise in USD import-price index
→ roughly 0.5-0.8 pp on CPI YoY over 3-6 months, and 0.3-0.5 pp drag on
GDP over 2-3 quarters. **2026 pencils as a smaller-than-2022 hit because
base-effect is helpful and KRW imp-price gain is similar but smaller
relative to base**.

## Q4. Supplementary budgets vs tax receipts — 2026 vs history

**Fiscal data (BOK SNA, annual, KRW trn)**:

| Year | Revenue | Expenditure | Net lending | Tax-Income | Tax-Production | Supp budgets |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | 523 | 504 | +19 | 122 | 164 | 1 (Jun) |
| 2018 | 648 | 591 | **+57** (peak) | 179 | 194 | none |
| 2019 | 670 | 651 | +18 | 179 | 197 | 1 |
| 2020 | 686 | 739 | **−53** (COVID) | 176 | 207 | **4** |
| 2022 | 860 | 860 | 0 | **268** (peak) | 234 | 2 |
| 2023 | 837 | 857 | −21 | **229** (−14.5% YoY) | 216 | 0 |
| 2024 | 859 | 896 | **−37** | **210** (−8.4%) | 227 | 1 |
| 2025 | n/a | n/a | n/a | n/a | n/a | 1 (announced Q2) |
| 2026 | n/a | n/a | n/a | n/a | n/a | New govt running |

**Headline narrative**: Korea ran the largest **two-year income-tax
shortfall on record in 2023-24** — receipts fell from 268 trn → 210 trn
(−22% in two years), against trend. This is the corporate-Samsung-
earnings collapse plus property-tax-revenue collapse from the 2022-23
housing crash.

**2026 vs past episodes**:
- 2025–26 fiscal-stance is comparable to 2020-21 — running modest
  deficits ~2-3% of GDP, with extra supplementary budgets to support
  consumption (Lee government's June 2025 winning campaign promised
  KRW 30 trn supp).
- BUT — the tax receipts side is more vulnerable than 2020 because the
  corp-profit base is concentrated in chips: while Samsung/SK Hynix are
  now booming (2026 chip super-cycle), the **tax lag is 1-2 years** (corp
  tax paid in March for prior year). So 2026 fiscal year still reflects
  2025 earnings — strong, but not yet at 2022 peak.
- The fiscal "pre-funding" episode equivalent to 2022 is now: government
  likely will under-collect vs supp-budget pace if global growth weakens.
  This is the hidden 2026-Q4 risk.

**JPM (5-Jun) "Local elections do not imply policy change"** specifically
flagged Lee's fiscal expansion pre-baked. So no fiscal *surprise*
expected in 2026, but **the structural deterioration in tax/GDP ratio
that started 2023 has not been fixed**.

## Q5. Seoul housing + jeonse → nationwide feedback, lags + betas

**Time-series facts (REB weekly indices, rebased to 2025-03-31 = 100):**

| Episode | Seoul Jeonse | Nat Jeonse | Seoul/Nat beta |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 Q3 (post-Moon boom) | 109.4 | 110.8 | jeonse near parity |
| 2022 Jan peak | 113.1 | 115.6 | Nat slightly higher (regional tail) |
| 2023 May trough | **91.4** | 96.4 | Seoul **−19% peak-to-trough vs Nat −16.6%**, beta ≈ 1.15× |
| 2024 Aug recovery | 98.0 | 99.1 | rebound, beta ≈ 1.2× |
| Feb 2026 latest | 104.1 | 101.7 | Seoul **+4.1% YoY vs Nat +1.7%, beta ≈ 2.4×** |

**Mortgage-loan flow corroborates**: BoK housing-loan stock 832 trn (Oct
2024) → 916 trn (Mar 2026) = **+10% YoY**, the steepest 18-month run
since 2020-21. Meanwhile non-housing HH loans are *contracting* (469 →
419 trn) — pure mortgage-driven re-leveraging.

**Lead-lag profile**:
- **Seoul leads Nationwide by ~4–6 weeks** in both up and down cycles.
  The 2022 crash: Seoul rolled over in late Sep 2022 (~111.7 peak Aug →
  110.5 Oct 24), Nat lagged 4 weeks (115.3 → 113.5).
- **Seoul jeonse leads Seoul sale by ~6–8 weeks** in the up-cycle (jeonse
  is supply-driven by contract roll, sale by speculation that follows).
  In the 2026 rally, jeonse has run +4% YoY before sale prints
  (parabolic-rally context per Barclays 28-May).
- **Seoul → Nationwide pass-through beta**: cycle-dependent. ~1.1-1.2×
  in down cycles, **~2.4× in current up-cycle** — the regions outside
  Seoul are *not* tagging along this time, which makes the rally
  fragile.

**Feedback loop into CPI / BoK**: jeonse rolls into the CPI rent
component with a **~12-month lag** (because rent baskets reflect rolling
2-year contracts). So Seoul jeonse +4% YoY in early 2026 will press CPI
services/housing through 2027. BoK already sees this — Barclays' "stars
are aligned" note (28-May) explicitly flags housing-credit + jeonse + FX
as the three reasons hike risk has materialised.

**Beta vs interest rates**: Seoul apt sale price has historical beta of
**≈ −5 to −7 to a 100bp BoK move** with 6-9 month lag (peak 2022→trough
2023 was a 22% Seoul fall on 250bp of cumulative hikes). The current
rally is happening *with* 200bp of cuts since Oct 2024 — exactly what
the cycle says should happen.

## Q6. Sector reaction order — hike vs cut cycles

Working from the **2021 Aug → 2023 Jan hike cycle (50bp → 350bp call
rate)** and the **2024 Oct → 2025 May cut cycle (3.55% → 2.50%)**:

**Hike-cycle reaction order (from BoK call-rate moves to GDP component
prints):**

| Order | Sector | Lag from first hike | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | **Construction investment** | 1–2 quarters | Down sharply (peaked −10% YoY by 2022) |
| 1st | **Goods imports (BoP volume)** | 1 quarter | Down (CA blew out on imp compression) |
| 2nd | **Facilities/capex** | 3-4 quarters | Down moderately (−2% YoY by mid-23) |
| 2nd | **Household credit flow** | 2-3 quarters | Stopped expanding 2022 Q3 |
| 3rd | **Private consumption** | 4-6 quarters | Slowed from 4% to ~1.5% YoY |
| 4th | **Headline GDP / employment** | 6-8 quarters | Moderate slowdown |
| Last | **Services PPI / Core CPI** | 8-12 quarters | Sticky — barely moved |

**Cut-cycle (Oct 2024 → May 2025, 105bp of cuts):**

| Order | Sector | Lag from first cut | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | **Housing-mortgage flow** | ~3 months | Re-accelerated (10% YoY by Mar 2026) |
| 1st | **Seoul jeonse prices** | ~3-4 months | Bottomed, then +4% YoY by Feb 2026 |
| 2nd | **Facilities investment QoQ** | ~6 months | Bounced +4.8% in 2026Q1 |
| 2nd | **Equity (KOSPI parabolic rally)** | ~6 months | Rally accelerated 2026 Q2 |
| 3rd | **Private consumption** | ~9 months | Still tepid in Apr 2026 (per Nomura/Barclays "weaker than expected") |
| 3rd | **Construction investment** | **>12 months** | Still −1.4% YoY in 2026Q1 |
| Last | **Headline CPI services** | Hasn't shown up yet | Core stuck 2.0-2.5% |

**Transmission asymmetry: cuts reach housing fast (rate-sensitive
collateral), capex moderately, and the real-economy demand legs
(consumption, construction) take well over a year.** Hikes reach
construction first because it's most credit-elastic.

**Sectors that react LAST in both directions**: services CPI, employment,
headline GDP. Korea's policy transmission to the real services-sector
demand cycle is notably slow vs to asset markets — a classic
small-open-economy with housing-collateral-channel pattern.

**Where 2026 sits**: late-cut cycle. Housing already responding (Seoul
jeonse +4%, mortgage stock at ATH growth rate). Capex turning.
Consumption still soft. If BoK now reverses and starts hiking in July
(which markets price), the asset legs will roll over first — Seoul
apt-sale beta is highest. Consumption / construction would lag the new
hike by another 6-12 months as usual.

## What to push back on / verify next

- **Household saving rate per se** isn't in our DB (only general-
  government gross saving). For the *S* side of S−I, the CA balance +
  BoK HH credit are the cleanest proxies — both point to the same
  story.
- **Supplementary budget historical count** quoted is from public
  knowledge — not in IMDR; we should add a `dim_indicator` event log
  for supp budgets if Q4 matters for ongoing work.
- **Oil price (Brent)** isn't directly in our econ DB — BoK import-price
  USD used as proxy. Adding FRED's `DCOILBRENTEU` to the econ ingest
  would let us compute clean lag-regressions.
- **Inflation expectations 1Y survey** (BOK.CCI.EXP_INFL) we have but
  didn't pull here — Nomura's 22-May "Stable inflation expectations
  reduce…" chart-alert leans on this. Worth folding in for Q2/Q6.
