Indonesia · Fiscal Deficit + SBN Investor Base

Indonesia fiscal & SBN — funding-pressure deep-dive

Who owns Indonesia's bonds, how has it changed, and what's driving the IDR / rates pressure right now — read against the 20-May BI hike, the 4-Jun UU P2SK passage, and the IDR break of 18,000.

Backfill note. This MD was authored on 2026-06-09 after the HTML render at indonesia-fiscal-sbn.html to retroactively become the source-of-truth markdown for that HTML under the Mycroft contract. Every claim has been re-grounded against IMDR live as of 2026-06-09. Discrepancies between the HTML and the re-verified data are noted in §10 Backfill notes.

01 TL;DR

  • The biggest investor-base shift in any Asian EM since 2015: foreign holders of tradable IDR-denominated SBN have fallen from 38.2% of stock at end-2015 to 12.6% at 2026-06-05 (Q1 in §5 below), replaced almost one-for-one by Bank Indonesia (10.2% → 26.7%) plus a captive domestic build (insur+pension 11.7% → 20.4%, retail 2.9% → 8.0%).
  • BI is now doing two contradictory things on the same balance sheet: a +50bp FX-defence hike on 20-May took policy to 5.25% (Q5, R-487/214) — yet by 4-Jun USD/IDR had still broken 18,000 for the first time (Q3, R-3538); meanwhile BI's net SBN holdings hit IDR 1,834tn / 26.7% of total (Q1), a record.
  • The pressure is external-twin-deficit driven, not inflation: Q1 BOP swung to a $9.1bn deficit vs Q4 surplus, CAD widened to $4.0bn (R-1253). Nomura sees CAD reaching 2.0% of GDP in 2026 vs 0.1% in 2025.
  • The mechanism inside the domestic stack: BI's SRBI sterilisation paper yields rallied 4.65% → 6.69% (+200bp in <1y) (R-1300), pulling IDR 240tn out of bank-balance-sheet SBN over six months. BI then plugs the rotation — net additions of ~+330tn over the same window (Q1).
  • Conviction: medium-high bearish IDR / bearish INDOGB front-end into 18-Jun BI meeting; underweight INDOGB 2-5y; consensus 25bp hike, but Barclays #1770 flags rising 50bp risk. Watch for parliament-level UU P2SK fallout — that changes the kind of trade.

03 Context

  • Fiscal anchor: UU 17/2003 caps the deficit at 3.0% of GDP. The cap was suspended 2020-22 via Perppu 1/2020 (COVID waiver), then restored from 2023. 2024 realisasi: revenue 2,851tn, expenditure 3,360tn, deficit IDR 509tn ≈ −2.3% GDP (Q2). The deficit has run between 1.6% and 2.6% of GDP every year ex-COVID; the funding profile is the story, not the headline number.
  • SBN stock: total tradable IDR-denominated SBN reached IDR 6,882tn at 2026-06-05 (Q1), ≈ USD 422bn at 16,300 IDR/USD. Stock has grown from 1,462tn (2015) at roughly 12% CAGR — slightly faster than nominal GDP.
  • BI's policy regime: 7-day reverse repo + corridor (deposit / lending facility). Pre-hike corridor 3.75% / 4.75% / 5.50%; post-hike 4.25% / 5.25% / 6.00% (Q4 — BI SEKI I.25.A May-26 row confirms 4.25 / 6.00 corridor endpoints). Above-corridor sterilisation is SRBI (Sekuritas Rupiah Bank Indonesia), launched 2023, now a major tool.
  • Recent regime shifts: Fitch lowered Indonesia's outlook to negative 4-Mar-2026 (referenced in R-229, R-3787); UU P2SK financial-sector law revisions passed by parliament 4-Jun-2026 expanding BI's mandate to "growth + jobs" + parliamentary evaluation of BI officials (R-3787).

04 Drivers

4.1 · External pressure — twin-deficit fundamentals worsened

The Q1 BOP swung from a +$6.1bn surplus in Q4 to a −$9.1bn deficit (R-1253, chunk 0). The current account deficit widened from −$2.5bn (Q4) to −$4.0bn (Q1) (R-1253). Net FDI fell from $3.2bn to $2.0bn; portfolio inflows collapsed from $4.7bn to $0.7bn. This is the underlying cause — "financial account weakened, led by net 'other investment' swinging back to a deficit, reflecting resident outflows" (R-1253, chunk 0). Nomura flagged "upside risks" to the 25bp June call on the basis of this print.

4.2 · BI's surprise 50bp hike — FX defence, not inflation

20-May: BI raised BI 7D-RR by 50bp to 5.25%, first hike since 2022, plus corridor widening (lending +50bp to 6.0%, deposit +50bp to 4.25% — Q4 confirms via SEKI I.25.A). BI's own words: the move was "a further step to strengthen the stabilization of the IDR from the impact of high global turmoil due to the war in the Middle East" (R-214, chunk 0); framed as "pre-emptive" on inflation. No vendor reads this as inflation-driven — it is unanimously read as FX defence (consensus: 5/5 desks; see §6).

4.3 · The SRBI sterilisation regime is pulling banks out of SBN

This is the mechanical driver of the domestic rotation. SRBI is BI's sterilisation paper. Blended yield rallied from a low of 4.65% in Oct-25 to 6.69% as of 22-May-26 (+200bp in <1 year) (R-1300, chunk 0). Bank holding of SRBI grew to roughly 70% of outstanding ("redirecting excess liquidity into high-yield BI instruments" — R-1300). The mechanical consequence: bank balance-sheet rotated out of SBN into SRBI. Bank holding of SBN dropped from ~1,484tn at 10-Dec-2025 (last clean weekly obs before the year-end rollover) to 1,245tn at 5-Jun-2026 — a decline of ~240tn over six months (Q1, Q-rollback). At the same time foreign holding of SRBI fell to IDR 192tn (~20% share) by Apr-26 from the Oct-24 peak (R-1300), i.e. foreigners rotated out of BI's own paper too.

4.4 · BI as residual buyer — the quasi-fiscal absorber

The accounting identity: new SBN supply + bank rotation out = BI absorbs. BI net SBN holding rose from ~1,500tn (early-Dec-25) to 1,834tn at 5-Jun-26 — net additions of +334tn over six months (Q1, Q-rollback; the HTML cites +216tn against an end-2024 reference of 1,618tn). The HTML's phrasing of "+216tn in six months" appears to mis-anchor against end-2024; the actual six-month-ago figure is closer to early-Dec-25. Either way, the qualitative point is intact: BI net holding is at a record share (26.7%) and a record absolute (1,834tn).

4.5 · Institutional risk premium rebuild

Two events compound the structural backdrop:

  • Fitch outlook to negative, 4-Mar-2026 (R-229 footnote, R-3787 — direct IMDR validation of the rating action sits outside the chunks; the citation is via vendor commentary).
  • UU P2SK financial-sector law revision passed 4-Jun-2026 — "The revision expands BI's mandate to include both economic growth (i.e., real sector) and job creation" (R-3787, chunk 0). Plus 17 other provisions including DPR evaluation power over BI officials. Nomura's framing: "Legislative changes, including expanding BI's mandate, pose an additional risk, if those changes are viewed by investors as potentially eroding BI's monetary policy independence" (R-3787, chunk 0).
  • Prabowo's state export agency ($65bn / 4.5% GDP of palm oil + coal + iron alloy re-routed through Danantara, effective 1-Sep-26): "a 'marketing facility', aiming to strengthen export supervision, curb 'under-invoicing' and prevent the embezzlement of proceeds of natural resource exports" (R-229, chunk 0). Nomura's read: "raises further investor concerns about greater state control, increase policy uncertainty, which is already leading to credit rating pressures" (R-229, chunk 0).

4.6 · Bank-sector OCI sensitivity

Nomura #199 sized the OCI hit per bank. BBCA holds the largest absolute SBN portfolio ("IDR425.6tn" — R-199, chunk 0) yet screens at only −0.41% OCI ("well-insulated… a testament to superior duration management and hedging discipline" — R-199, chunk 0). BBNI screens worst at −2.92% ("driven by large mark-to-market losses on a mid-sized SBN portfolio – the worst risk-adjusted outcome in our coverage" — R-199, chunk 0). OCI hits don't flow P&L but compress book value / Tier-1. Another 50bp hike at 18-Jun pushes long-end yields ~30bp higher per Nomura and BBNI's Tier-1 becomes the watch-item.

6.8 · Disagreement + counterintuitive

  • Disagreement on supply absorption. Barclays #3378 is sanguine ("more supply but nothing overwhelming"). Nomura #1300 sees the SRBI/bank-bid squeeze as fragile.
  • Counterintuitive #1 — The non-BI non-foreign share has fallen in 2026 H1: banks 1,485tn (end-Dec-25) → 1,245tn (Jun-26), an IDR 240tn rotation out of SBN (Q1, Q-rollback). BI is plugging an actual domestic rotation, not just absorbing net new supply.
  • Counterintuitive #2 — BI hikes the policy rate but simultaneously encourages banks not to raise lending rates (R-1770). The hike doesn't transmit to bank-lending margins; SRBI's +200bp is the true cost of marginal liquidity.
  • Counterintuitive #3 — Foreign exit runs through SRBI too: foreign SRBI holding fell from Oct-24 peak to IDR 192tn (~20% share) by Apr-26 (R-1300) — foreigners are leaving anything IDR-denominated, not just sovereign credit.

07 Mycroft's view

Conviction: medium-high bearish IDR / bearish INDOGB front-end into the 18-Jun BI meeting. State the bias, name the trade:

  • FX: Long USD/IDR (or long SGD/IDR per Nomura's max-conviction framing in R-214) into 18-Jun. Triple-intervention works at the margin but the trend is one-way given (a) Q1 BOP collapse, (b) corporate USD-dividend demand into July-Aug (R-3538), (c) the institutional risk-premium rebuild (UU P2SK, Fitch outlook). Stop at 17,500; near-term target 18,500.
  • Rates: Underweight INDOGB 2-5y. ANZ targets 12M-5Y at 7.0-7.5% in 3-6 months (R-3538); the bear-flattener trade is consensus but still has room. SRBI marginal funding cost (6.69% and rising) anchors the short end.
  • Sovereign credit: No view yet — Barclays #3378's "bring on the supply" framing is defensible at current spreads. The trade is not on USD INDON; the trade is on the IDR-rates / IDR-FX side where the marginal balance-sheet user (BI) has constraints.
  • Banks: Sector underweight bias driven by BBNI OCI sensitivity. If 18-Jun delivers 50bp, BBNI Tier-1 becomes the proximate risk-asset story.

The bigger argument I'm running: the SBN investor-base shift makes Indonesia less market-priced and more policy-priced than at any time since 2015. With foreign at 12.6% (vs 38% historical), the marginal price-setter for IDR-rates is now BI itself plus regulated domestic captives (insur+pension at 20.4%, banks via SRBI). That means the price signal from auction cover ratios or 2Y yield prints is weaker than it used to be — but the quantity signal (BI's balance sheet running, SRBI stock expanding) is louder. Watch the quantity, not the price.

Where I'd be wrong: a credible signal that the post-UU P2SK BI remains operationally independent (e.g. another 50bp at 18-Jun without political pushback), combined with Iran-war de-escalation, would collapse the institutional risk premium fast. The trade is asymmetric: the bull case requires two exogenous things, the bear case requires only one driver to keep going.

08 What would change my mind

  1. BI delivers 50bp at 18-Jun without political pushback. Signals operational independence intact post-UU P2SK; collapses one of the two pillars of the institutional risk premium. Re-think IDR short.
  2. Iran-war ceasefire / oil drops below $70. Removes the BOP-side shock; flips IDR positioning rapidly given crowded short.
  3. DJPPR weekly print shows foreign re-engaging — foreign share back above 14%. Mechanical buying capacity from foreign re-entry is ~1,000tn over 2-3 years; a single weekly print >+10tn net inflow would suggest a turn.
  4. Bank SBN holding stabilises or rises week-on-week. Would mean the SRBI crowd-out has run its course and the marginal bank balance sheet is back at the SBN bid.
  5. APBN 2027 (submitted to DPR August) anchored at 2.5% deficit or lower. Eases pre-funding pressure into early-2027; removes the "wider deficit" trigger Barclays #3378 names as the rating-action condition.
