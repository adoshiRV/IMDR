## The day in brief

No policy decision moved in the universe over the weekend, and the three prints that dominate the week — **US June CPI (14-Jul), China Q2 GDP + activity (15-Jul), Bank of Canada (15-Jul)** — are all still forward. So Friday's close into Monday's open was a **pure repositioning move, and it ran the opposite way to last week's**. The USD firmed against the entire coverage set (AUD −0.31%, IDR −0.28%, THB/JPY −0.25%, INR −0.20%, SGD/EUR −0.19%, MYR −0.18%, GBP −0.17%, CAD −0.15%; NZD ~flat, HKD +0.03% on the peg), and the front end and belly **backed up** rather than rallied — NZD 2y +7.1bp, HKD 2y +6.1bp, PHIREF 2y +4.85bp, SORA 2y +3.0bp, THOR 2y +2.9bp, AONIA/TONAR/SOFR ~+2bp. The 07-10 run's spine (a dovish, richer-curve, softer-USD move into a soft-CPI expectation) has partially unwound. The market is now pricing a *hiking* Fed into the print — ANZ's OIS read shows +22bp by the 16-Sep meeting and +38bp cumulative by December, with parallel hawkish repricing across the BoE (+29bp by Dec), ECB (+34bp), BoC (into 2027) and RBNZ (+49bp).

Two forces drove it, and both are about inflation being stickier than the oil chart suggests. First, **geopolitics re-escalated**: the US and Iran each ran new attacks last week, the Strait of Hormuz is "unresolved" (HSBC 17678), and Trump said talks continue but the ceasefire is "over" (DB 17668) — Nomura's short-term FX-theme model (17715) flipped its dominant signal to **OIL** on 13-Jul, and HSBC warns that a reconnected USD-oil-geopolitics link "would give the broad USD a fresh source of support." Second, the **inflation-stickiness camp gained voice**: Barclays (17692, "The oil shock has faded. Inflation has not.") RAISED its core PCE forecast to 3.3% Q4/Q4 — 50bp above where it started the year — *even as oil fell*, on AI-driven memory-chip and data-centre-power inflation, a lack of demand destruction (record travel/retail), and the six-to-eight-quarter lag with which oil shocks bleed into core; BNP (17655) holds core at 0.37% and favours Dec-26/Mar-27 FOMC steepeners; GS's FX desk (17620) carries an "above-consensus bullish USD" call; and DB's podcast argues "recent events help the case for Fed hikes." Against them, GS economics (17669/17700) still forecasts **core CPI +0.17% / core PCE +0.24%** with the Fed on hold through 2026 — but even GS concedes the path "leaves little margin for error."

Note the tape's internal split: **equities (the freshest close is 07-10, one session behind Monday) rose broadly** — Nikkei +1.20%, Nifty +1.02%, SIMSCI +0.73%, SET +0.70%, HSI +0.60%, ASX +0.50%, S&P 500 +0.42% (DB: +1.2% on the week, still ½% below the 2-Jun record) — so the equity leg was still risk-on into Friday, even as the Monday FX/rates open leaned defensive/higher-yield/stronger-dollar. The one place the *rates* story is domestic, not a CPI derivative, is Japan: TONAR 10y +4.0bp with the GPIF-reallocation chatter (BNP 17655) and Saravelos's "yield is the only thing driving FX" frame (DB 17668) both feeding a long-USD/JPY carry view.
