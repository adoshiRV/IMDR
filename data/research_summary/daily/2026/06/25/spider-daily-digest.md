### Australia

Heaviest domestic flow of the day: two policy-relevant prints back-to-back (May CPI on 24 Jun, May labour + household spending on 25 Jun) landed straight into a live hike-vs-hold debate for the 11 Aug RBA meeting.

#### A. Themes in play

| Rank | Theme | Puzzle piece | What moved it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reaction function: Aug hike vs hold | CB reaction function | May CPI + labour; RBA's "a little tight" framing |
| 2 | Inflation composition: headline vs underlying | Inflation | Headline 4.0% on fuel; trimmed mean *rose* to 3.6% |
| 3 | Oil relief lowering the inflation path | Cross-market flows | Fast Brent fall cuts Q4-26 CPI forecasts |
| 4 | Labour-market slack emerging under the surface | Real economy | +40.3k jobs but April revised to −40.7k; hours −1.1% |
| 5 | Fiscal support to growth (budgets, data-centre capex) | Fiscal / stimulus | State/Federal budgets confirmed supportive |

#### B. The "why"

The tension every AU desk is wrestling with (SYN): headline and underlying inflation are moving in *opposite* directions. FACT — headline CPI eased to 4.0% y/y in May (from a 4.6% March peak) entirely on collapsing fuel (April −7% m/m, May −12%, June tracking −9%), while the trimmed mean *rose* 38bp on the month to 3.6% y/y, the fastest since Q3-24 (`econ.fact_indicator` corroborates: headline 4.0%, trimmed mean 3.6%, weighted median 3.6%, with housing at 6.5% and new-dwelling costs re-accelerating to 5.6% y/y). VIEW — UBS reads this as underlying inflation "still unacceptable for the RBA" and keeps an Aug +25bp call to 4.60%, but flags it "now a closer call" after cutting its Q2 headline forecast to 0.8% q/q on fuel.

On labour (FACT), the +40.3k May gain flattered a soft picture: April was revised from −18.6k to −40.7k, hours worked fell 1.1% m/m, and annual employment growth (+1.0%) still runs behind population (+1.8%). VIEW — ANZ argues the data "won't sway" the RBA and, with Q2 trimmed mean tracking a touch *below* the RBA's forecast, holds its Aug-hold call. The May household-spending pop (+1.3% m/m) is discounted by ANZ as pull-forward and price-driven, with little real-volume growth. The reaction-function uncertainty is itself a theme (VIEW, GS): the 7 external MPB members don't publicise views, so Governor Bullock's June tightening bias is the main readable signal.

#### C. Consensus views

- **Shared claim (UBS + GS):** the RBA still leans toward one *final* hike in August, contingent on a still-hot Q2 trimmed-mean CPI. **Evidence:** trimmed mean +38bp m/m to 3.6% y/y; market services ~0.9% q/q with "no signs of easing"; minimum/award wage rises (+6% / +4.75%) from 1 July. **What consensus is missing (SYN):** both concede the call hinges on a single print (Q2 CPI, 29 Jul) they haven't seen, and both had to *cut* their headline paths on oil — so the "hike" consensus is thinner than the headline suggests and could flip fast.
- **Shared claim (all desks):** oil's fast fall has lowered the near-term inflation *path*. **Evidence:** UBS Q2 headline cut to 0.8% q/q; GS cut 4Q26 CPI by 27bp to 4.2%. **What's missing:** the fuel-excise extension (16c vs full 32c) merely *spreads* the July fuel spike into August rather than removing it — a mechanical bump most desks flag but the market may under-weight.

### Japan

Richest thematic flow. BoJ hiked to 1.00% on 16 Jun (FACT); the day's notes unpack how far and how fast it goes from here, plus the fiscal (JPY370trn) and FX-intervention overlays.

#### A. Themes in play

| Rank | Theme | Puzzle piece | What moved it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | BoJ hike path: pace & terminal | CB reaction function | Tamura speech; June MPM Summary of Opinions |
| 2 | JPY370trn public-private investment plan | Fiscal / stimulus | 23 Jun Growth Strategy Council announcement |
| 3 | JPY & second-round intervention | Currency | USDJPY near defence zone; Katayama–Bessent contact |
| 4 | Above-neutral gap / inflation anchoring | Real rates | Tamura: policy still below ~2% neutral |
| 5 | Tokyo CPI / underlying inflation | Inflation | Tokyo core ex-fresh-food 1.6% (26 Jun) |

#### B. The "why"

The BoJ debate has shifted from *whether* to *how fast* (SYN). FACT — the policy rate is 1.00% after the 16 Jun hike. VIEW — Barclays (Baba) reads Policy Board member Tamura's 25-Jun speech as "considerably more hawkish" than the already hawkish June MPM Summary: Tamura assumes a ~2% neutral rate, wants hikes "once every few months," and would "accelerate without hesitation" on upside inflation. Crucially Barclays *keeps its own path unchanged* — Oct + Apr-27 hikes to a 1.5% terminal — explicitly discounting Tamura as one of the two most hawkish members whose views diverge from the more conservative executive leadership. This is the model house-treatment: a persistent-conviction hawk is filed as a *board tail*, not the modal path.

On fiscal (VIEW, Barclays), the JPY370trn (FY26–FY40) headline needs heavy discounting: inflation-adjusted it is ~JPY321trn (~JPY21.4trn/yr), only ~20% is government public support, and much is funded by bridge JGBs excluded from fiscal metrics — so the true fiscal-deterioration impulse is closer to ~JPY1trn/yr after existing plans. Nomura and SocGen carry the same blueprint with a growth-strategy/longer-term-fiscal-projection lens. On FX (VIEW, Citi/Takashima), the government has *deferred* a second intervention round; Citi keeps ¥160–162 as the likely trigger band with an aim of ¥155–157, and notes the relatively orderly rise in long rates despite yen weakness may be *lowering* the government's priority on JPY defence — a reflationary Takaichi administration is not unhappy with a soft yen.

#### C. Consensus views

- **Shared claim (Barclays + Nomura + MS):** the BoJ continues a gradual, interval-based hiking cycle, but executive leadership is more conservative than the hawkish wing. **Evidence:** Tamura's own framing of hikes "at intervals of a few months"; MS titles a note "Rate Hike with Intervals of a Few Months." **What consensus is missing (SYN):** the terminal-rate anchor is unsettled — Barclays holds 1.5%, Tamura implies ~2.0% — so the *level* the curve should price is genuinely contested, not just the timing.
- **Shared claim (Barclays + Nomura + SocGen):** the JPY370trn plan is far less fiscally expansive than the headline. **Evidence:** inflation-adjustment to ~JPY321trn; ~20% public share; bridge-JGB funding off the fiscal ledger. **What's missing:** the plan may later be *re-defined* to fold part of the investment into defence-spending metrics — a definitional risk that could revive the fiscal-supply concern the desks are currently downplaying.

### United States

Biggest single reaction-function shift in the window: the June FOMC held at 3.50–3.75% (18 Jun, FACT) but the dots moved *up* and desks are now modelling a *hiking* cycle — an unusual stance to open a US read with.

#### A. Themes in play

| Rank | Theme | Puzzle piece | What moved it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "New Fed" + upward dots → hike debate | CB reaction function | June SEP: current-yr dot 3.4→3.8%; 1st-yr 3.1→3.6% |
| 2 | Core PCE vs CPI divergence | Inflation | May core PCE 0.3% m/m; 3m annualised 3.5% |
| 3 | Growth: Q1 GDP revised up but demand soft | Real economy | Q1 GDP 2.1% (3rd); PDFP/consumption revised down |
| 4 | Oil reprieve capping near-term fixings | Cross-market flows | Hormuz flows resuming; energy reprieve |
| 5 | Real-yield curve under a hiking Fed | Rates / curve | BofA RY-flattener thesis |

#### B. The "why"

The US read is now built around *tightening*, not easing (SYN). FACT — the June SEP dots shifted materially higher (`cb_events`: current-year projection 3.4→3.8%, 1st-year 3.1→3.6%, 2nd-year 3.1→3.4%, longer-run unchanged 3.1%). VIEW — BofA's midyear reset explicitly changed its Fed call and now expects a *modest hiking cycle* focused on returning core PCE to 2%, with Mark Cabana's rates team building a real-yield-curve flattener around it. The inflation split is the crux: FACT — May core PCE rose 0.3% m/m (3m annualised 3.5%, off a ~4% start-of-year handle), while core *CPI* is better-anchored. VIEW — Barclays flags the PCE miss was driven by BEA-imputed "nonprofit institutions" deflation and a stock-market-linked financial-services boost that will fade; it keeps Q4/Q4 core PCE at 3.3% and is "skeptical" of the soft software/memory-price print given elevated PPI/import pipeline pressure. On growth, FACT — Q1 GDP was revised up 0.5pp to 2.1% q/q saar, but VIEW (Barclays) reads the *down* revision to the bellwether PDFP and consumer spending (Q1 real consumption cut to +0.5% saar) as the real signal — "gradual deceleration in underlying core demand," downplaying reacceleration.

The oil overlay (VIEW, Citi/HSBC): the Hormuz de-escalation and "energy reprieve" cap near-term inflation fixings, motivating Citi's short Dec-26 inflation fixings. So the US puzzle is a genuine tension: sticky PCE + higher dots pulling toward hikes, soft demand + oil relief pulling the other way.

#### C. Consensus views

- **Shared claim (BofA + Barclays + Citi + SocGen):** the Fed's operative target is core *PCE*, which remains too high and is the reason cuts are off the table. **Evidence:** core PCE 3m annualised 3.5% vs better-anchored CPI; dots up. **What consensus is missing (SYN):** the hawkish read leans on components (financial-services fees, imputed nonprofits, memory prices) that several of the *same* desks admit are noisy or fading — so the "sticky PCE" case is partly built on series they themselves distrust.
- **Shared claim (Barclays + MS + JPM):** headline Q1 GDP strength (2.1%) overstates momentum; underlying demand is softening. **Evidence:** PDFP and Q1 consumption revised down; continuing claims trending higher. **What's missing:** none of the desks reconciles *softening demand* with *higher dots* into a single coherent path — the growth-dovish and inflation-hawkish reads sit side by side unresolved.

#### D. Differentiated / unique views

| Bank | Asset | View | Why different | Hidden assumption | Falsifier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BofA | US rates | Modest Fed *hiking* cycle → real-yield-curve flattener | Only desk building a positioned RY-flattener off a hike call | Fed prioritises PCE over softer CPI + soft demand | Fed holds/cuts on demand deceleration |
| Barclays | US rates | Skeptical of the soft PCE; Q4/Q4 core PCE held at 3.3% | Fades its *own* dovish print on pipeline PPI/import pressure | Memory/software price pressure re-emerges in PCE | PCE stays soft for 2–3 more months |

### Indonesia

BI hiked +25bp to 5.75% on 18 Jun (FACT), capping a rapid tightening run (4.75%→5.25%→5.50%→5.75% since late April). The day's flow is a currency-and-flows story.

#### A. Themes in play

| Rank | Theme | Puzzle piece | What moved it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | IDR trajectory after the hikes | Currency | IDR +2% vs EUR; DXY / hawkish-Fed correlation |
