## The week in brief

Hold the regime the whole street is still trading: the **March 2026 US–Iran conflict** spiked crude, broadened inflation, and turned nearly every central bank in our universe hawkish. The prior week had produced the first genuine *doubt* about the US leg — a soft +57k jobs print and an oil round-trip to ~$69.5. **This week that doubt was taken back.** On **8 July President Trump declared the Iran ceasefire over**; WTI spiked above $76 intraday and Brent brushed **$80** (Citi: +8–9% in days; the US also revoked the waiver on new Iranian oil sales). The reaction was a broad **DM bond selloff** — US 2y +8bp / 10y +9bp, and UK and German 2y up ~15bp with European breakevens widening sharply (Barclays). The hawkish repricing that had wobbled the week before came straight back.

The exception, tellingly, was **Japan**. The 10y JPY swap *fell* 6bp to 2.57% and 30y JGBs round-tripped a sharp intraday sell-off to close roughly unchanged — the fiscal-supply premium that spiked the prior week cooled as the Takaichi government's food consumption-tax cut got *delayed* (no consensus with the opposition; likely pushed past the summer budget requests) while the "no-upper-limit" growth-investment framework and the incorporation of supplementary budgets into the initial budget stayed. Japan's data still argues for the next hike (bank lending +5.7% y/y, real lending rates still negative — DB's point that the easing is more accommodative than the 1.0% policy rate implies), but the JGB curve traded *fiscal relief*, not the hike, this week.

The delivered decision was **RBNZ (8 July)**: a 25bp hike to **2.50%**, reached by consensus without a vote, with hawkish-but-data-dependent guidance ("some further reduction in monetary stimulus is likely to be required"). ANZ and JPM both keep hiking calls — ANZ to 3% by late October, JPM +25 in September then +50 more into early 2027. A June manufacturing PMI that *leapt to 59.7* (from 51.3, top-5% in 24 years) validated the turn; the NZD and NZ front end led the universe higher (2y +15bp). The UK moved almost as much on the global rates wave alone (10y/2y +12–13bp) despite soft domestic data.

EM-Asia kept splitting on the **oil-shock barbell**. On the strained side: the **Philippines** (headline eased to 6.4% but core *rose* to 4.4% — UBS keeps a +25bp August call, BSP's Remolona says the economy can absorb one) and **Indonesia** (rupiah the softest ASEAN FX at ~18,068 as the government widened its 2026 deficit target to 2.85% of GDP on higher subsidies). On the resilient side: **Malaysia** (BNM held, "firmly" in the 4–5% growth range on stronger exports, Citi biased to a stronger MYR and BNM on hold to 2027) and **Singapore** (2Q GDP tracking ~5.6%, tech-led, into a genuinely close MAS call). **Thailand** printed a CPI *miss* (2.4% vs 2.8%) that cements a longer BoT hold even as DB flags the basic balance-of-payments turning against the baht. **Hong Kong** equities ran +5–6% (HSTECH +6.0%) on the North-Asia semis/China beta. **India** sits apart on the June FX-package "summer of hope" (HSBC: ~$65bn potential inflows, buy IGB 15y, sell USD-INR) — but that thesis was built on *lower* oil, and the ceasefire broke two days after HSBC published; DB is more cautious (inflation risks > growth, monsoon 15% below normal), and the June CPI lands **today**.

The trades that express the week: **pay US 5y5y SOFR** (Barclays' resilience-plus-term-premium call, the near-consensus DM expression); **short the twin-deficit importers** (IDR, THB) into the oil re-spike; **long KTB / received KRW rates** into July supply relief (DB, Citi, though Korea sits outside our roster); **long-end IndoGB** (Citi constructive vs DB *sidelined* — an unusually clean two-desk split); and India's **buy IGB / sell USD-INR** package trade. And the calendar is a wall: **US CPI + MAS + SG GDP (14 Jul)**, **BoC (15 Jul)**, **BoK (16 Jul, 25-vs-50)**, then **BI (22 Jul)**, **AU Q2 CPI + FOMC (29 Jul)**, **BoE (30 Jul)** and **BoJ (31 Jul)**.

## The big cross-cutting themes

**1. The ceasefire breaks — geopolitics retakes the wheel.** The single pivot of the week: on 8 July Trump declared the Iran ceasefire over, the US revoked the waiver on new Iranian oil sales, WTI spiked >$76 and Brent brushed $80 (Citi: +8–9%). That reversed the prior week's oil round-trip and drove a broad DM bond selloff (US 2y +8bp, UK/Germany 2y ~+15bp, European breakevens widening — Barclays). Corroborated across Nomura (Nikkei fell below 66,000 then recovered; every $10 oil crimps TOPIX EPS ~1.0–1.25%), Citi and MS (Hormuz weekly tracker #101). What breaks it: a fresh, durable ceasefire (oil back to ~$70, dovish re-repricing) or, the other way, a Hormuz disruption that takes oil decisively through $85. *Sources: 17078, 17115, 17170, 17203, 17103.*

**2. Resilience vs "the doubt" — and the CPI that adjudicates.** The US data kept "keeping on ticking" (JPM): FOMC *held* in June with upside inflation risks flagged and downside labour risks abated; initial claims 215k, ISM services 54.0, existing home sales the only soft spot. Barclays pays 5y5y on resilience and a structural "bond glut"; Nomura forecasts benign June core CPI (~0.215% m/m, y/y core 2.9%, core PCE 3.4%) and **no hikes through 2027**. The split is *how sticky hike-pricing is*, not direction — and the **14 July CPI + Warsh's first testimony** settle it. What breaks it: a hot core (>0.3% m/m) reopens the June hike-pivot premium; a soft print revives the cut debate. *Sources: 17602, 17555, 17115, 16129.*

**3. Behind-the-curve BoJ meets a fiscal premium that *cooled*.** Japan's data still says hike (bank lending +5.7% y/y, real lending rates negative, Tankan capex firm, machinery orders AI-driven), but this week the JGB curve *rallied* (10y OIS −6bp) because the Takaichi food consumption-tax cut was **delayed** (no opposition consensus) while the growth-investment framework and supplementary-budget incorporation stayed. GPIF's FY25 results show rebalancing *into* JGBs from equities (Barclays), a structural bid. The live risk is the mid-July Cabinet approval of the Basic Policy: lax final wording re-ignites the term premium. What breaks it: a credible funded plan (yields lower) vs a no-discipline signal (super-long re-sells). *Sources: 17209, 17115, 17078, 17502.*

**4. The North-Asia AI/semiconductor cycle is still the connective tissue — but wobbling.** Hong Kong's HSTECH +6.0% and MSCI Singapore +5.6% are the region's China/semis beta; the cycle drives HK, SG, KR and TW together. But MS ("semis lose momentum") and Nomura (active Japanese funds de-beta-ing, "correcting the AI/semiconductor skew" ahead of Apr–Jun earnings) both flag a stall. It is a *price/nominal* effect as much as volume. The arbiter is **US Q2 earnings, now starting**. Shared falsifier for the whole complex: a semis rollover. *Sources: 17355, 17078, 15763.*

**5. EM-Asia's oil-shock barbell hardens (North surplus vs South twin-deficit).** DB's "Challenges for H2" frames it: the tech cycle drives divergence *between* (North vs South) and *within* (K-shaped) economies. Strained importers — **Philippines** (core to 4.4%, +25 August likely), **Indonesia** (deficit to 2.85%, softest FX), **Thailand** (basic BoP turning) — fight oil with rates or FX; surplus/tech winners — **Malaysia** (BNM on hold, stronger-MYR bias), **Singapore** (tech-led GDP) — run calm policy. Positioning has turned received across INR/KRW/SGD/TWD; the sharp two-desk split is IndoGB (Citi long-end constructive vs DB sidelined). *Sources: 17174, 17203, 17291, 17170.*

**6. India — "summer of hope" vs an oil re-spike and a rising CPI.** HSBC turned broadly constructive (FX package ~$65bn inflows, +INR6tr liquidity, FY27 GDP raised to 6.3%, buy IGB 15y / sell USD-INR) on the premise of *lower oil* and eased financial conditions. Two days later the ceasefire broke and oil re-spiked; DB is more cautious (June CPI ~4.16%, FY27 CPI 4.9%, monsoon 15% below normal, "inflation risks higher than growth"). The June CPI (today) and the pace of promised inflows gate the complex. *Sources: 16316, 16948, 17386.*

**7. The DM curve back-up and the "bond glut."** Barclays' structural thesis — persistent fiscal deficits + a price-sensitive buyer base shift the balance "from a global savings glut to a bond glut," pointing to higher long yields, amplified in the US by a Fed balance-sheet review that keeps ample reserves but accelerates portfolio-duration reduction (~60bp of suppressed term premium at risk). This week's synchronous DM sell-off (US/UK/CA/NZ all +7–15bp) is the cyclical expression of a structural story; JGBs are the standout counter-move. *Sources: 17115, 17578, 17425.*

**8. The calendar wall — a fortnight of live decisions.** US CPI + MAS + SG GDP (14 Jul), BoC (15 Jul), BoK 25-vs-50 (16 Jul), BI (22 Jul), then AU Q2 CPI + FOMC (29 Jul), BoE (30 Jul), BoJ (31 Jul). Several are coin-flips (MAS steepen-vs-pause, BoK 25-vs-50); two are cycle-deciders (US CPI for the Fed, AU CPI for the RBA). Positioning into a wall this dense is itself the risk. *Sources: cb_events; 16797, 17259.*

### Driver 2 — The oil re-spike undercuts the premise

HSBC published on 7 July on an explicit *lower-oil* premise ("two of three concerns eased — oil has fallen"); the ceasefire broke on 8 July and oil re-spiked toward $76–80. Spot ground to 95.41 (INR one of the softer Asian FX on the week) despite the package.

> **BOOK-LEVEL NOTE** — The HSBC package trades (buy IGB 15y, sell USD-INR) were built on a lower-oil, easing-conditions premise that the 8 July ceasefire break directly undercut. The structural inflow thesis is Solid; entering long-INR / long-IGB into a re-spiking barrel and a rising June CPI (today) is poorly timed. Let the CPI + the first inflow data print before adding — the falsifier for the *timing* is exactly the oil path.

**Position implication:** defer adding the HSBC package trades until the June CPI and inflow pace confirm; the oil re-spike is the near-term headwind to both legs.

### Driver 4 — Fiscal: growth-supportive, still on track

DB's fiscal read: April–May FY27 deficit ran INR1.62tn (9.6% of the full-year Budget Estimate, vs 0.8% same period last year) on front-loaded spending, weaker tax (excise cut) and subsidy pressure — but capex rose +13.4% y/y to INR2.51tn and non-tax revenue was boosted by the RBI's record dividend. DB judges the FY27 deficit target of 4.3% of GDP still achievable (lower oil, the RBI dividend, the INR1tn Economic Stabilization Fund backstop, higher nominal GDP). The subsidy watchpoint (fertiliser +47%) is the swing if oil stays high.

> **BOOK-LEVEL NOTE** — The fiscal picture is the quiet support under the India bull case: growth-supportive capex + a record RBI dividend keep the 4.3% deficit target credible even with front-loaded spending. But it is oil-contingent — the fertiliser/fuel subsidy math (DB) deteriorates if crude holds above $75, the same channel that pressures the INR. Fiscal and FX are one oil-linked risk here.

**Position implication:** the fiscal backdrop supports the bonds structurally, but its oil-contingency links it to the same near-term headwind as the FX — reinforcing the "wait for the CPI + oil to settle" stance on the package trades.
